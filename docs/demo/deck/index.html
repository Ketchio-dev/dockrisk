DockRisk · the problem02

The dock costs a city desk twiceand only one of them shows up in the accounting

The visible loss

Detention that is never billed

The truck waits past the two-hour free allowance. Nobody wrote down the in and out times, so there is no defensible claim — and the carrier eats it. Every TMS on the market can compute this after someone types the timestamps in. Nobody types them in.

The invisible loss

The next load

A driver can have driving hours left while the dock wait eats the 14-hour on-duty window. The next pickup becomes infeasible before the free time even runs out — so the desk finds out when the driver calls, hours after it could have been fixed.

The brief calls automated detention billing the critical requirement. We took the bonus question seriously instead: what happens when a driver runs out of hours sitting in a dock queue?

0:40–1:30. 비대칭이 목적이다. 보이는 손실은 회계 문제, 안 보이는 손실은 운영 문제. 마지막 줄은 주최측 브리프의 보너스 질문을 그대로 인용한 것이다.

읽을 것 — 아래 영어를 그대로

A dock delay costs a city desk twice.

The first cost is detention. Somebody can see it, because there is a number on an invoice.

The second cost is the load the driver can no longer legally take. Nobody sees that one. It never becomes a line item.

Your own brief asks how a delay at one stop changes the rest of the day. That is your question, not ours. This is our answer.

DockRisk · where the model is allowed11

Two AI moments. The engine computes every number.both labelled in the UI · both with a deterministic fallback

1 · Read the rate confirmation

A carrier's rate con becomes structured detention terms — free time, rate, increment, clock-start rule — with the clause quoted under each field, so the dispatcher confirms against the contract, not against the model.

2 · Draft the customer notice

Written from the evidence packet that already exists. Every figure in the draft is checked against the engine's own output before it is shown.

The rule we did not break: the model never produces an amount, a duration or a deadline. When the endpoint is absent the rules path runs and the product keeps working.

11:40–12:10. Best Use of AI는 특별상인데, 정직한 프레이밍이 더 큰 모델보다 자주 이긴다. 심사위원은 오늘 LLM이 자신만만하게 금액을 지어내는 데모를 여섯 번쯤 봤다.

읽을 것 — 아래 영어를 그대로

There are exactly two places we use a model, and both are labelled in the product.

One reads a rate confirmation and pulls out the terms. It shows the sentence it used, so you check the contract, not the model.

The other drafts the customer notice, and then checks its own draft against the packet before anyone can send it.

The rule is one line. The model reads and writes. It never counts.

Rules do the law and the money. Both paths keep working with the model switched off.

전송 전 확인 목록을 가리켜라. 시스템이 자기 초안을 스스로 반박하고 있다 — 10초 값어치 한다.

DockRisk · the desk's own rules12

Detention terms are per customer, so they are data — not codefree time · rate · increment · clock-start rule · on-time requirement

Every carrier we read about argues the same four things with every shipper: when the clock starts, how long is free, what it costs, and whether the driver has to have been on time. So those are fields, editable at the desk, versioned, and replay-safe — change the policy and the historical replay recomputes rather than rewriting the past.

Of the 257 charges drafted from the export, 225 had an appointment on file and would start the clock at the appointment; 32 would start it at arrival. Same fleet, same month, two different rules — which is exactly why this cannot be hard-coded.

12:10–12:35. 조용한 슬라이드인데 "우리 계약은 다 다르다"는 반론을 누가 말하기 전에 답한다.
시간이 밀리면 제일 먼저 잘라낼 슬라이드가 이것이다.

읽을 것 — 아래 영어를 그대로

Detention terms are different for every customer. So we made them data, not code.

Free time, rate, increment, when the clock starts.

Two hundred and twenty-five of the stops in your export fall under one set of terms. Thirty-two fall under another.

Change the terms, and every charge behind them recomputes. Nobody edits a rule file.

225 대 32 분할은 추출본에서 나온 실제 숫자다. 논증보다 이게 더 잘 증명한다.

DockRisk · the limits13

What we did not build, and whynamed in the product, not just in this deck

Axle-weight compliance

The Trucks sheet has exactly one column — a truck number. There are no specs in the file, so the check is not buildable. Trailer-capacity checks are, and those we do.

131 of 131 rows

Leg-level hours of service

Our first "finding" was that the export's leg HOS was dangerously stale. It failed its own test: it is a frozen per-driver copy, not a decision field. We kept the retraction in the repo and join hours from the driver sheet instead.

100%

Charges against a city centroid

Where we only have a city point rather than surveyed dock geometry, the charge is drafted but flagged needs review and never billed silently. s.76 adverse-driving relief is surfaced as "possible, review required" — never applied automatically.

to review

No rates or revenue exist anywhere in the export, and 68% of legs carry a 1980 sentinel instead of a planned date. Financial impact is modelled from dwell and distance, and labelled as modelled, everywhere it appears.

12:35–13:10. 직관에 반하지만 이 슬라이드가 점수를 번다. 심사위원은 하루 종일 "다 됩니다"를 들었다. 안 되는 것 세 가지를 이름 붙여 말하는 게 가장 빠른 신뢰다.

읽을 것 — 아래 영어를 그대로

Here is what we did not build, on purpose.

Axle weight. Your truck sheet has one weight column. There is nothing to compute a load plan from, so we did not pretend.

We do not report revenue. The export has no rates.

And stops over six hours never bill themselves. Thirty-eight of those stops — one and a half percent — carry half of all the detention hours in the file.

We also found a bug in our own reading of your data. We were wrong about it, we fixed it, and we wrote down that we were wrong. It is in the repo.
